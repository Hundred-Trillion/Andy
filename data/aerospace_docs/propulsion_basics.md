# Rocket Propulsion Fundamentals

## Thrust Equation
The thrust generated by a rocket engine is:
F = ṁ × Ve + (Pe − Pa) × Ae

Where:
- ṁ = mass flow rate (kg/s)
- Ve = exhaust velocity (m/s)
- Pe = nozzle exit pressure
- Pa = ambient pressure
- Ae = nozzle exit area

## Specific Impulse (Isp)
Specific impulse measures engine efficiency:
Isp = F / (ṁ × g₀)

Typical Isp values:
- Solid motors: 200–280 s
- Liquid bipropellant (LOX/RP-1): 300–350 s
- Liquid bipropellant (LOX/LH2): 420–460 s
- Ion engines: 1500–5000 s

## Nozzle Design
Convergent-divergent (de Laval) nozzles accelerate exhaust to supersonic speeds:
- Throat area determines mass flow rate
- Exit area ratio determines expansion and exit velocity
- Optimal expansion: Pe = Pa (perfectly expanded nozzle)
- Under-expanded: Pe > Pa (common at altitude)
- Over-expanded: Pe < Pa (potential flow separation at sea level)

## Propellant Types
- **Solid**: APCP (ammonium perchlorate composite). Simple, reliable, not throttleable.
- **Liquid Bipropellant**: LOX/RP-1, LOX/LH2, N2O4/UDMH. Throttleable, restartable.
- **Hybrid**: Solid fuel + liquid/gaseous oxidizer (e.g., HTPB + N2O). Simpler than liquid, throttleable.
- **Monopropellant**: Hydrazine, hydrogen peroxide. For attitude control thrusters.
